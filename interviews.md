# Interview Transcripts — Wishlist → Purchase (n=6)

Conducted 2026-08-20 by Mayank. Verbatim notes. **Coded blind in Phase 4 — the engine had NOT run
at coding time, so the coding is genuinely uncontaminated.** These are the evidence base for the
per-claim audit (see `interview_audit.py`, which verdicts the top register claims against them).

> n=6 is qualitative — patterns, not proportions. Main-driver tally across the 6:
> **Price 2 · H3 (save ≠ intent) 2 · H2 (occasion decay) 1 · Attention/forgetting 1 · H1 as the primary blocker: 0.**

---

## R1 — Aarav, 26, Software Engineer — "The Intentional Buyer"
**Item:** Black oversized jacket
- Shops with a need (trip/wedding). Searches, checks reviews, saves a few, buys one but not immediately.
- Wishlist = shortlist of things he might buy. ~30–40% converts.
- Saved the jacket for a Manali trip; liked it but ₹2,800 felt expensive → saved to wait for a sale.
- What happened: price dropped a little, not enough; got busy; trip neared; wore something he already had.
- Real intent? "Yeah, seriously considering. But at that price, not fully convinced."
- Ask: set a target price, notify when it hits.
- Last wishlist buy: sneakers ₹3,000 → ₹1,900 in a sale, bought immediately. Sat ~2 months.
- **Signal:** genuine intent, **price** delayed conversion (occasion decay second).

## R2 — Riya, 22, College Student — "The Maybe Someday Collector"
**Item:** Green cargo pants
- Sometimes shops, sometimes just scrolls Myntra like Instagram when bored.
- Wishlist = a collection of things she likes. ~10% or less converts.
- Saw similar cargos on Instagram → searched → saved; liked colour/fit.
- What happened: forgot; now unsure she even likes them.
- Real intent? "If honest, probably not. Just thought they looked nice."
- Ask: show old saved items and ask if she still likes them — but "a notification alone wouldn't make me buy."
- Last wishlist buy: a party top she'd saved; went back and bought. Sat ~3–4 weeks.
- **Signal:** wishlist is **inspiration (H3)**, not a purchase funnel.

## R3 — Kunal, 29, Marketing Manager — "The Comparison Shopper"
**Item:** White sneakers
- Knows what he wants; searches, looks at many options.
- Wishlist = all the options he's considering; comparison; avoids re-searching. ~20% converts (buys 1 of 5–6).
- Saved this pair among 7–8; design good, reviews decent.
- What happened: found another he liked more, bought that. This stays because he doesn't need another.
- Real intent? "Not necessarily [this item]. Definitely going to buy sneakers, but this was just one candidate."
- Ask: select wishlist items and compare side-by-side (price, rating, material, delivery).
- Last wishlist buy: a shirt, from 4 saved options after comparing reviews. Sat ~2 weeks.
- **Signal:** intent exists at the **category level (H3 comparison)**, not per individual product.

## R4 — Sneha, 24, Consultant — "The Event-Driven Shopper"
**Item:** Blue dress
- Shops when something triggers it (wedding, vacation, birthday).
- Wishlist = save options for an event, decide later. ~15–20% converts.
- Saved the dress for a friend's wedding; thought it could work.
- What happened: found another she liked more, bought that; wedding over; never removed this.
- Real intent? "Considering, yes, but not committed."
- Ask: organise by occasion (Wedding/Vacation/Workwear); otherwise it becomes one huge random list.
- Last wishlist buy: a co-ord set for a Goa trip — trip was close, needed to order, fast delivery. Sat ~2 weeks.
- **Signal:** **context expiration (H2)** kills conversion.

## R5 — Rahul, 27, Financial Analyst — "The Discount Hunter"
**Item:** Nike sneakers
- Rarely buys immediately; waits for discounts.
- Wishlist = keep products until a sale; price tracking. ~20–30% converts, discount-dependent.
- Liked the sneakers immediately but ₹4,500 too expensive → saved.
- What happened: price dropped a couple times, not enough; once his size was unavailable. Still there; "might eventually lose interest."
- Real intent? "Yes, but only below a certain price. At ₹4,500, no chance."
- Ask: set a target price; notify only when it actually reaches it.
- Last wishlist buy: a hoodie during End of Reason Sale — big discount + size might sell out. Sat ~3 months.
- **Signal:** conversion needs **price + availability + urgency** together.

## R6 — Priya, 31, HR Manager — "The Forgotten Wishlist User"
**Item:** Beige shirt
- Browses at night / in free time; saves interesting things to avoid deciding in the moment.
- Wishlist = things to look at later; a reminder. Converts "very little"; has old items she doesn't remember saving.
- Saved the shirt but had no time to check reviews or the size chart.
- What happened: completely forgot. "If you hadn't asked me to open my wishlist, I wouldn't remember it existed."
- Real intent? "Interested, but hadn't decided."
- Ask: remind her about recently-saved items — usefully (show 3, ask if still interested), not random notifications.
- Last wishlist buy: re-searched a product, realised she'd already saved it; bought because she actually needed it then. Sat ~1–2 months.
- **Signal:** the problem is **attention/forgetting**, not lack of desire. (Note: she skipped reviews/size-chart, yet the blocker was forgetting — not fit/quality doubt.)
